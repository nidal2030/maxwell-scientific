Gather starter stamps
You can begin with inexpensive worldwide mixtures, beginner packets, or stamps from your own mail. Many collectors also trade duplicates with friends or join local clubs for low-cost variety.
Use proper tools
Invest in stamp tongs (to avoid damaging stamps with fingers), a magnifying glass, and a simple album or stock book. These tools protect your stamps and make organizing easier.
Learn how to mount and store stamps
Beginners should use stamp hinges for inexpensive stamps and stamp mounts for valuable ones. A good album keeps your collection neat and safe.
Study stamp details
Look at perforations, watermarks, printing methods, and variations. This is where the hobby becomes fascinating — small details often reveal big stories.

Handle stamps correctly
Always use stamp tongs. Oils from fingers can stain or weaken paper over time.
Store stamps in a safe environment
Keep your albums in a cool, dry place away from sunlight. Heat and humidity can cause stamps to curl, fade, or stick together.
Protect stamps from dust and moisture
Use archival-quality albums and keep them closed when not in use. Avoid basements, attics, and areas prone to moisture.
Avoid adhesives and tape
Never use glue or tape on stamps or album pages. These cause permanent damage and reduce value.
Check your collection periodically
Inspect for signs of mold, fading, or stuck stamps. Regular care keeps your collection healthy for decades.
Keep stamps organized
Label sections, maintain a simple catalog, and store duplicates separately. Organization makes collecting more enjoyable and prevents accidental damage.
